The six truthsnobody opens with

You should hear these from us, before you buy anything from anyone.

Truth 01
A readable file is a copyable file

File systems have no "read but don't take". Secrets never live in this structure, only references to your secret manager. Design boundary, not a footnote.

Truth 02
An agent's permissions are infrastructure, not promises

Anyone telling you their agent "is safe" by design is selling a story. The fences live in your platform: protected branches, CODEOWNERS, read-only scopes. We configure them with you; your infrastructure enforces them.

Truth 03
Human gates don't scale by themselves

Review gates are the trust feature and the first bottleneck. Every gate ships with an owner, a queue and an SLA, or it becomes a gate people walk around.

Truth 04
It only knows what got written down

The context install doesn't make the model smarter — it hands it your recorded answers. What nobody recorded, the agent still fills with a confident guess. Getting the right knowledge out of the right heads is most of the work; that craft is what you're actually buying.

Truth 05
The bench measures what the bench measures

A build is judged on what its protocol registered: catches, recall, cost. Anything beyond the judge — "the output is just better" — is a claim we refuse to make, and anyone making it without a mechanical judge is guessing. Jobs where the numbers say don't build, we refuse; those receipts are published.

Truth 06
A delivered agent ages

Your code moves; the context it was built on ages, and an outdated answer lies with confidence. Keeping an agent true is an ongoing discipline, not built-in magic — the Day-30 drift report shows exactly what moved, and you decide if it's worth maintaining.
